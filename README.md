# Goal Mountain

Keep a habit going while you work toward one big goal. Plan your week, tick things off, watch your skills level up, and climb a mountain one step at a time.

**Try it:** https://rokaveni.github.io/goal-mountain/

## Before you start: please read

**This is a motivation and progress tracker, not a learning app.**
It doesn't teach you anything and has no lessons or exercises. It helps you show up consistently and see your effort adding up, whatever you're working on. I used it for Finnish, but you can make it work for any big goal.

**I'm not a developer.**
My original system is fully manual: I colour in progress bars by hand and keep track of time myself. After my LinkedIn post about passing the YKI test got more attention than I expected, people asked how I did it, so I used Claude to turn that system into something interactive that anyone can use. That means it has limitations, and I don't plan to keep developing it beyond small tweaks.

**Your data stays in your browser, and only there.**
- Nothing you type is sent anywhere. There are no accounts and no tracking, and there's no AI running inside the app. I can't see your data, and neither can anyone else.
- The flip side: your progress only exists in the browser you use. If you open the page on another device, in another browser or in a private window, you'll start from zero. Clearing your browser data erases it.
- To be safe, download a backup file now and then from *Settings → Backup*. Loading that file on another device moves your progress there.

## How it works

1. **Pick your pace** in *Settings*: how many hours a week you can give your goal, on whichever days suit you (weekdays, evenings, only weekends). The mountain is sized to match, so it feels about the same whether you have 2.5 or 20 hours a week. The mountain rewards consistency, not bursts of effort that aren't sustainable. Your pace then locks, so you don't accidentally reshuffle your progress.
2. **Name your goal and add your categories**: the areas you want to work on (for a language, maybe listening, speaking, reading, writing and grammar). You don't need 5 categories, you can have more or less, do whatever works for you.
3. **Plan your week** on the *Climb* tab: pick a day and add what you want to do, like "Grammar, 30 min, textbook". The plan repeats every week, and a line under it shows roughly how fast you'll climb at that pace.
4. **Tick things off** as you do them. The minutes go straight into that category's level tracker ring.
5. **Level up.** When a ring fills, that category levels up and your hiker climbs one step up the mountain.
6. **Reach checkpoints.** Every 10 steps (about once a month at your planned pace) you hit a checkpoint and get reminded to reward yourself. You can write down how you'll reward yourself in advance in the *Rewards* tab, but you can also leave it empty and decide when you get there. You can also keep track of reward ideas in the *Rewards* tab.
7. **Log extra time** in *Log* when you did something that wasn't on the plan.
8. **Reach the summit.** You get a choice: extend this mountain with more steps and keep climbing with your current levels, or start a brand-new mountain for your next goal (you can keep your categories and weekly plan). Finished goals are kept in a *Summits reached* list in *Settings*.

The app opens with example data so you can see how it looks and test how it works. Today's list includes demo tick boxes you can tick to see a level-up, a checkpoint and the summit. Press **Start fresh** to begin with your own goal.

## Good to know

- On your phone, use your browser's *Add to Home Screen* option to save it like an app.
- Each category levels up on its own, so the more categories you have, the slower progress can feel at the start.
- Changing your pace mid-climb recalculates every level from your logged time, so your hiker may move up or down. That's why the pace settings are locked until you choose to unlock them.
- Removing a category also removes the time logged for it.
